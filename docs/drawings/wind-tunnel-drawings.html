Findings from drawing it out

Gaps that only showed up when the parts were placed together. Recorded in PROJECT_STATUS.md §11 (2026-11-09); none is resolved yet.

  1. Settling-chamber length grows with the chosen parts. The docs’ 230–300 mm “full decay” length assumed 0.15–0.25 mm wire and 2–3 mm cells. With the chosen window screen (0.010–0.011 in wire: 500 d = 127–140 mm) and 6 mm printed cells (≥150 mm after the honeycomb), the same rules give about 380–390 mm plus frames. Mehta1979’s 0.2 D spacing gives about 200 mm. Our arithmetic; decide at sizing.
  2. Taps “one per wall” meet the lift-off panels. The top panel (and possibly a side panel) covers the E and M stations, so taps there would have to be unplugged at every model swap. Options: taps only in fixed walls, or panels that stop short of the stations.
  3. The M-station floor tap cannot sit at the floor centre. The sting hole and shield are there. The floor tap needs an offset (drawn 30 mm upstream, TBD) or the station uses three taps. It now sits in the sphere insert (53), so its tube is unplugged whenever the insert comes out.
  4. 250 mm sections need splitting in cross-section, not only along the flow. A 250 mm square duct plus walls and flanges is wider than the 256 mm P1S bed, so each chamber and contraction-inlet segment must be printed in halves or as flat panels (D-005 covers segments along the flow only).
  5. The balance box sets the tunnel’s height. At layout values the box hangs to about 158 mm below the centre line, lower than the chamber’s flanges (142 mm). The test-section feet are therefore the tallest, and the fan stand must match the resulting centre-line height.
  6. I²C length limits where the Arduino box can go. With the BME280 near the fan inlet and the SDP810 near the test section, each run under about 1 m puts the Arduino box near the middle of the tunnel.
  7. Turning the sting-lock knob twists the load cell. The twist from the thumb screw (68) goes through the mount into the 100 g cell unless the mount is held. The screw is drawn along the wind, so any twist that gets through bends the cell in its stiff sideways direction (if the sensitive axis is as assumed on sheet 8). Hold the mount through the insert opening while turning. Our reasoning; not quantified.
