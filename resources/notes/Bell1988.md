## What they did

They built a procedure for *evaluating* (not directly generating) a chosen contraction wall shape for small,
low-speed tunnels, checked it against four existing tunnels, and used it to pick the wall shape for a new NASA Ames
mixing-layer tunnel.

- **Procedure** (pp. 9–13):
  1. A 3-D potential-flow panel code (VSAERO; 1010 panels on a quarter model, with straight ducts extending 0.5 L
     upstream and 1.5 L downstream) gives the wall velocity and the exit-plane uniformity (10 × 10 grid; standard
     deviation and max − min).
  2. A 2-D laminar boundary-layer calculation along the centreline of the curved wall, by Thwaites' integral method,
     takes the wall velocity as input. Thwaites agreed with a finite-difference code (PDMINT) to within 10 % near the
     exit.
  3. Separation is flagged when the skin-friction coefficient is negative or very low (C_f ≤ 0.0005, p. 13).
- **Key assumption** (pp. 11–12): the boundary layer starts laminar, from stagnation, at the contraction inlet and
  stays laminar throughout. Justification: screens relaminarize the incoming boundary layer (Mehta 1985), and the
  strong favourable pressure gradient of a c = 6–10 contraction relaminarizes it again if it transitions.
- **Validation** (pp. 13–14, Tables 1–2): exit momentum thickness θ was predicted for four existing blower tunnels and
  compared with measurements. All were within about 10 %, with predictions generally low. The 2-D calculation misses
  the weak secondary flow that converging sidewalls drive toward the centreline.
- **Wall-shape selection** (pp. 14–15, Table 4, Fig. 14): for the new mixing-layer tunnel (c = 7.7, 2-D, 15 m/s),
  they compared 3rd-, 5th- and 7th-order polynomials and matched cubics, and varied the length.

## Key numbers

Scope and rules:

- "Small, low-speed" means a test-section area below about 0.5 m² and speeds below about 40 m/s. Contraction ratios
  of 6 to 10 are adequate for such tunnels (p. 4).
- A square or rectangular section is acceptable: without separation, corner flows stay local (p. 4, citing Mehta 1978).
- Typical flow-quality target: less than 1 % velocity variation outside the boundary layers (p. 5).
- Downstream of the contraction exit, velocity non-uniformity decays roughly as exp(−2πx/H_e), falling to 10 % of its
  exit value by x = 0.37 H_e (p. 5, from Morel 1976). H_e is the exit height.
- Wall curvature: concave at the inlet destabilizes the boundary layer (Görtler); convex at the exit stabilizes it.
  Adverse pressure gradients sit near both ends and get stronger as the contraction gets shorter (pp. 4–5).

Batchelor's (1953) rapid-distortion factors for an axisymmetric contraction (p. 3). These are factors on *absolute*
levels; the text says lateral intensities are "enhanced" in absolute terms. Our arithmetic gives the factors on
intensity relative to the mean (÷ c):

| Quantity | Factor (as printed) | c = 6.25 | c = 7.7 | c = 9 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Streamwise mean-velocity variation | 1/c | 0.160 | 0.130 | 0.111 |
| Lateral mean-velocity variation | √c | 2.50 | 2.78 | 3.00 |
| u′ (streamwise r.m.s.) | [3(ln 4c³ − 1)]^½ / (2c) | 0.336 (u′/U: 0.054) | 0.287 (0.037) | 0.254 (0.028) |
| v′, w′ (lateral r.m.s.) | (3c)^½ / 2 | 2.17 (v′/U: 0.35) | 2.40 (0.31) | 2.60 (0.29) |

Wall equations (p. 14). X′ = X/L, H_i and H_e are the inlet and exit heights, Y is the wall height at X:

- 3rd order: Y = H_i − (H_i − H_e)[−2X′³ + 3X′²]
- **5th order:** Y = H_i − (H_i − H_e)[6X′⁵ − 15X′⁴ + 10X′³]
- 7th order: Y = H_i − (H_i − H_e)[−20X′⁷ + 70X′⁶ − 84X′⁵ + 35X′⁴]
- Matched cubics, match point χ. For X′ ≤ χ: Y = H_e + (H_i − H_e)[1 − X′³/χ²]. For X′ ≥ χ the printed branch is
  H_e + (H_i − H_e)[1 − X′³/(1 − χ)²]. That form does not reach H_e at X′ = 1 and does not join the first branch, so it
  is probably a misprint. (H_i − H_e)(1 − X′)³/(1 − χ)² joins the first branch in value and slope at χ and reaches
  H_e (our check).

The 5th-order shape has zero slope *and* zero curvature at both ends, and its inflection is at mid-length (X′ = 0.5)
(our check). Fig. 13 (p. 35) plots all four shapes.

Table 4 (p. 24): wall shapes at c = 7.7, L/H_i = 0.89, 15 m/s. Uniformity is the standard deviation over the exit
plane. Units are not stated; it is probably a fraction of the mean, which fits the text's "better than 1 % variation"
for the 5th order.

| Wall shape | Separation predicted | Re_θ at exit | Exit uniformity (std. dev.) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 3rd-order polynomial | No | 359 | 0.0102 |
| **5th-order polynomial** | **No** | **428** | **0.0040** |
| 7th-order polynomial | Yes (inlet) | 478 | 0.0024 |
| Symmetric matched cubics | Yes (inlet) | 484 | 0.0024 |

- Matched cubics were tried with χ = 0.3, 0.5, 0.6, 0.7 and 0.8. Only χ = 0.3 stayed attached, but its exit boundary
  layer was too thick (Re_θ ≈ 600) (p. 15).
- Length (5th order, c = 7.7; p. 15, Fig. 14 on p. 36):
  - separated near the inlet at L/H_i = 0.667;
  - separated near the exit at L/H_i = 1.79, because a long contraction thickens the boundary layer;
  - minimum acceptable length was L/H_i = 0.89.

  Read from Fig. 14 (approximate), the centreline-velocity deviation is about 1.45 % at 0.67 (separated), 1.23 % at
  0.78, 0.95 % at 0.89, 0.78 % at 1.12, 0.48 % at 1.34 and about 0.05 % at 1.78 (separated).
- Chosen design: the 5th-order polynomial. It was free of separation "both on the centerline and in the corners",
  with Re_θ ≈ 400 and better than 1 % variation at 15 m/s (p. 15).
- A 40:1 smoke-tunnel contraction with L/H_i = 0.66 was predicted to separate at its inlet (p. 14).

## Limitations and caveats

- The authors limit their conclusions to: test section ≤ 0.5 m², speed ≤ 40 m/s, contraction ratio around 8, exit
  aspect ratio about 5, and L/H_i about 1 (p. 16). **A square section (aspect ratio 1) is outside that range.** They
  warn that the 2-D boundary-layer calculation may work less well for smaller aspect ratios and longer contractions,
  where 3-D effects grow (pp. 13–14).
- It is an evaluation method, not a direct design method, and the wall-shape choice was made for a single case
  (c = 7.7, L/H_i = 0.89, 15 m/s, a 2-D contraction with a splitter plate).
- The uniformity numbers are inviscid predictions. Only θ was validated against measurement.
- The separation criterion is laminar and applied on the curved-wall centreline. The paper says the 5th-order design
  was also free of separation in the corners but does not show how corners were checked.
- Internal inconsistencies:
  - Section 4.2 says the shape was chosen for "wind tunnel D", but later says it went on "wind tunnel (B)". Table 1
    supports B (5th order, c = 7.7, L/H_i = 0.89).
  - Table 1 gives B's length as 244 cm, which with a 137 cm inlet is L/H_i = 1.78, not 0.89. A length of 122 cm would
    give 0.89 (our arithmetic).
  - The matched-cubic misprint noted above.
- Abdelhamed2014 cites this report as "JIAA report TR-64". The cover of the JIAA edition says TR-84.

## Comparison with Abdelhamed2014 (our synthesis)

- **They agree on:**
  - contraction ratio of about 6–10 (Abdelhamed2014 quotes 6–9);
  - length about one inlet height (Bell1988: L/H_i 0.89–1.2 in the tunnels studied; Abdelhamed2014: L / inlet
    hydraulic diameter = 1);
  - a square cross-section being acceptable (both cite Mehta 1978);
  - concave-then-convex walls with flat ends.
- **They differ on where the wall turns over** (the inflection point). The 5th-order polynomial puts it at 0.5 L;
  Abdelhamed2014's optimum puts it at 0.41 L.
- **The objectives differ.** Bell1988 minimized length and exit boundary-layer thickness subject to no laminar
  separation. Abdelhamed2014 maximized exit uniformity at a fixed length, using RANS CFD.
- Neither paper shows that one shape is better under the other's criteria.
- **For building, the 5th-order polynomial has a practical advantage:** it is a closed-form equation that goes straight
  into CAD, and two built 5th-order contractions (B, C) were validated. Abdelhamed2014's profile needs a spline
  through digitized points.

## What it suggests for this project [Proposed]

Not decided. These are inputs to build step 1.

1. **Baseline wall shape: the 5th-order polynomial,** applied to the half-width of each wall of a square section,
   measured from the centreline: y(x) = h_i − (h_i − h_e)(6ξ⁵ − 15ξ⁴ + 10ξ³), with ξ = x/L and h_e/h_i = 1/√c.
   Abdelhamed2014's profile is the alternative to compare against.
2. **Length:** L ≈ 1.0 × inlet height for c ≈ 6–9. 0.89 was the bare minimum at c = 7.7, and a square section is
   outside the validated aspect-ratio range, so some margin above the minimum is sensible.
3. **Model position:** put the model at least about 0.4 exit heights downstream of the contraction exit (non-uniformity
   down to about 10 % of its exit value). The test-section length must allow for this.
4. **Keep the honeycomb and screens.** At c = 6.25 the contraction cuts lateral turbulence intensity only to about
   35 % of its inlet value (Batchelor factor), so flow conditioning (build step 2) still matters.
5. **Segmented printing (D-005):** the analysis assumes smooth walls and a laminar boundary layer from the inlet. Steps
   at joints, especially near the fast, thin-boundary-layer exit, work against that assumption.
6. **Possible later check:** Thwaites' method is simple enough to code in standard-library Python and check a chosen
   shape for laminar separation (C_f ≤ 0.0005). The harder part is the wall-velocity input, which needs a
   potential-flow solution. Not planned yet.
