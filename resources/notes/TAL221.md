## What it is

An aluminium-alloy parallel-beam ("straight bar") load cell, IP65, in capacities 100–1500 g. Its listed uses are
kitchen, postal and other small scales. Four-wire full bridge.

## Limitations and caveats

- Every error is given as % of full scale, measured over the full range. The datasheet says nothing about behaviour at
  1–3 % of full scale, which is where this project works.
- Creep is given over 3 minutes only.
- No natural frequency, stiffness or deflection at full scale.
- The datasheet does not say which axis is the sensitive one; the standard for a parallel beam is force across the
  bar, perpendicular to its long axis and its 12 mm face (our understanding; check on the delivered part with a known
  mass).

## What it suggests for this project [Proposed]

Our arithmetic, 2026-11-08, with the 100 g cell, a 3/4 in sphere and Eq. (19) drag (0.30 gf at 6.5 m/s, 1.27 gf at
13 m/s):

- **0.05 % FS = 0.05 g,** which is 4 % of the drag at 13 m/s and 17 % at 6.5 m/s if the full-scale figures held at
  our loads. Creep and hysteresis usually scale with the applied load, so at gram loads they should be far smaller,
  but that is not specified. **The load-cell calibration (build step 5) must measure repeatability and hysteresis at
  0.2–3 g.**
- **Zero drift with temperature does not shrink with load:** ±0.01 g per °C, which is 0.8 % of the drag per °C at
  13 m/s and 3.4 % at 6.5 m/s. Take a wind-off zero before and after every run, and keep the load-cell box closed and
  away from the fan motor.
- **Safe overload is 150 g.** A finger pushing a model onto the sting can exceed that, so the sting lock and overload
  stops are needed.
- The 100 g cell is the smallest in the family. A smaller cell with a datasheet was not found among cheap parts.
- Excitation ≤ 6 V suits an HX711 module powered from 5 V.
