## What it is

A 24-bit sigma-delta ADC with a low-noise programmable-gain amplifier (PGA), made to read a load-cell bridge directly.
It has an on-chip regulator that can power the bridge (with an external PNP transistor), an on-chip oscillator, and a
two-wire serial interface (PD_SCK clock in, DOUT data out). No registers: channel and gain are set by the number of
clock pulses. Fig. 1 (p. 1) shows the typical weigh-scale circuit: bridge excited from AVDD, outputs to INA+/INA−.

## Key numbers (p. 3, Table 2 unless noted)

| Parameter | Value |
|---|---|
| Channel A gain | 128 or 64. Full-scale input ±0.5·(AVDD/GAIN): ±20 mV or ±40 mV at AVDD = 5 V (p. 1, p. 4) |
| Channel B gain | Fixed 32; ±80 mV at AVDD = 5 V (p. 4). No noise figure is given for channel B |
| Input common-mode range | AGND + 1.2 V to AVDD − 1.3 V |
| Output data rate | 10 SPS (RATE pin = 0) or 80 SPS (RATE = DVDD) on the internal oscillator; with a crystal, f_clk/1,105,920 or f_clk/138,240 (11.0592 MHz gives exactly 10 or 80 SPS) |
| Output coding | 24-bit two's complement, 800000h to 7FFFFFh; saturates at those values when over range (p. 4) |
| Settling time | 400 ms (10 SPS) or 50 ms (80 SPS) after power-up, reset, channel change or gain change |
| Input offset drift | 0.2 mV (gain 128), 0.4 mV (gain 64). The table gives no time base or conditions |
| Input noise | 50 nV rms (gain 128, 10 SPS); 90 nV rms (gain 128, 80 SPS) |
| Temperature drift | Input offset ±6 nV/°C; gain ±5 ppm/°C (both at gain 128) |
| Common-mode and power-supply rejection | 100 dB each (gain 128, 10 SPS) |
| Reference bypass V_BG | 1.25 V |
| Supplies | DVDD 2.6–5.5 V; AVDD and VSUP 2.6–5.5 V (VSUP 2.7–5.5 V in the pin table, p. 2) |
| Supply current | Analog 1400 µA, digital 100 µA in normal operation; 0.3 µA and 0.2 µA powered down |

**Power** (p. 4):

- DVDD should be the same supply as the microcontroller.
- With the on-chip regulator, AVDD = V_BG·(R1 + R2)/R2, and must be at least 100 mV below VSUP. The resistors set
  the bridge excitation, so it depends on the module.
- Without the regulator: tie VSUP to the higher of AVDD and DVDD, VFB to ground, leave BASE unconnected.

**Serial interface** (pp. 4–5):

- DOUT goes low when a conversion is ready. Then 25–27 PD_SCK pulses shift out 24 bits, MSB first. Pulses 25–27 set
  the next conversion: 25 = channel A gain 128, 26 = channel B gain 32, 27 = channel A gain 64 (Table 3).
- Timing (p. 5): PD_SCK high time 0.2 µs min, 1 µs typ, **50 µs max**; low time 0.2 µs min; data valid 0.1 µs after
  the rising edge.
- Holding PD_SCK high for more than **60 µs** powers the chip down (Fig. 3). On power-up it returns to the last
  channel and gain setting.
- The reference C driver (p. 7) XORs the result with 0x800000 (converts two's complement to offset binary).

## Limitations and caveats

- A short datasheet: typical values only, no minimum or maximum for noise or drift, and no noise-versus-gain figure
  for channel B.
- "Input offset drift 0.2 mV" has no stated time or temperature basis. It is large compared with a load cell's
  full-scale signal of a few millivolts, so treat the zero as unknown until tared (our reading).
- Everything here describes the HX711 chip. Breakout modules differ in excitation voltage (their R1/R2), RATE pin
  wiring, grounding and layout. The module this project will use is not chosen.

## What it suggests for this project [Proposed]

Not decided. Inputs to choosing the load cell, microcontroller and firmware.

1. **The HX711 is adequate for gram-level forces if the load cell is sized for it.** Our arithmetic, with assumed
   values (5 V excitation; rated outputs as listed; the HX711's 50 nV rms at gain 128 and 10 SPS):

   | Load cell (capacity, rated output) | Full-scale signal | Noise per sample | Mean of 300 samples (30 s at 10 SPS), white noise only | HX711 offset tempco |
   |---|---|---|---|---|
   | 100 g, 0.6 mV/V | 3.0 mV | 0.0017 g | 0.0001 g | 0.0002 g/°C |
   | 1 kg, 1.0 mV/V | 5.0 mV | 0.010 g | 0.0006 g | 0.0012 g/°C |
   | 10 kg, 1.0 mV/V | 5.0 mV | 0.10 g | 0.006 g | 0.012 g/°C |

   - Expected sphere drag is about 1.9 to 7.8 gf for a 150 mm test section at 10–20 m/s
     ([PROJECT_BACKGROUND.md](../../PROJECT_BACKGROUND.md#printable-contraction-sizes), estimates).
   - With a 10 kg cell, single-sample noise is about 5 % of 1.9 gf. Averaging helps only if the noise is white. Real
     limits will be the load cell's own creep and temperature drift (not in this datasheet) and fan vibration.
   - A smaller cell gives proportionally less noise in grams, but less overload margin for handling. The 1.0 mV/V
     values are assumptions; read the chosen cell's datasheet.
2. **One HX711 per load cell.** Switching channel or gain costs 50–400 ms of settling, so multiplexing channels A and
   B is slow. Weakly2024 used one module per load cell.
3. **Firmware:** read DOUT only when it is low, and do not let an interrupt hold PD_SCK high longer than 50 µs (more
   than 60 µs powers the chip down). Log raw counts, not grams, so calibration stays in the analysis scripts
   (CLAUDE.md conventions).
4. **Sample rate:** 10 SPS has lower noise; 80 SPS shows vibration and vortex shedding better. Decide when the
   microcontroller is chosen. Record the RATE pin state with each dataset.
5. **Excitation and ratiometric reading:** the full-scale range scales with AVDD, so exciting the bridge from AVDD
   makes the reading ratiometric (our inference, not stated in the datasheet). Supply noise then largely cancels.
   Measure the module's actual excitation voltage once it is bought.
