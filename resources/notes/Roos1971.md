## What they did

- They towed a 7.62 cm (3 in) sphere, and separately a disk, on a sting through a 0.61 × 0.61 × 9.75 m channel
  (pp. 285–286, Figs. 1–2).
  - The fluid was a glycerine-water mixture, chosen to vary Re; plain water was used for the highest Re.
  - The carriage ran on air bearings, and drag was measured by a strain-gauge balance and recorded on strip charts.
- Steady sphere drag was measured "through most of the range 5 < Re < 100,000" (Table 1 runs to Re = 118,300). It
  was compared with a band of earlier data (refs. 6–13) in Fig. 3.
- They also measured drag on a sphere accelerating from rest and on the disk, and used hydrogen-bubble flow
  visualization.
- Blockage ratio (model to channel cross-section) 0.012. The data were **not** corrected for wall interference
  (p. 290).

- The data dots just above the band match the Table 1 values (0.46–0.53), and the authors say those points are
  "somewhat higher" than the earlier data. So the band cannot sit at 0.6, above the dots.
- Even so, a tabulated source should replace this figure reading (resources "Wanted" list).

**What the authors say:**

- Scatter in the existing data "amounts to at least 10% over much of the Re range" (p. 285).
- "The greatest scatter in the present data occurs at high subcritical Re (5500–100,000), where it also appears that
  the CD values are somewhat higher over-all than the existing data level" (p. 286).
  - Their explanation: almost all those points came from a shakedown test.
  - "Considerable mechanical vibration" made the drag records "difficult to evaluate with accuracy, particularly at
    low drag force levels."
- Scatter was much lower for 400 ≤ Re ≤ 2000, where an improved balance built for the sphere was used (p. 287).
- During steady motion, drag fluctuated by no more than about 5 % of the mean, with or without vortex shedding
  (p. 287; conclusion 3).
- **Sting effect:** the sting delayed the onset of wake unsteadiness, to Re ≈ 290, against Re ≈ 215 for a sphere hung
  on two threads. The authors say this "does not affect the agreement of the present CD data" with others' (p. 287).
- After a rapid start, drag overshot the steady value by up to 30 % at high Re until the wake settled (p. 288;
  conclusion 4).
- **Wall effects** (p. 290), for their 0.012 blockage, from Fidleris and Whitmore's falling-sphere study: drag rises
  by "close to 10%" at Re = 10 and by "less than 2%" for Re ≥ 100. For a disk, Maskell's theory gives less than 5 %
  for Re > 100. Neither was applied to the data.

## Limitations and caveats

- **At this project's Re (about 1–2.5×10⁴), Table 1 is at its weakest.** Those are the shakedown-test points that the
  authors say read high, with an SD of about 0.04 (8 %). Their means are 20–28 % above Morrison's correlation and at
  or above the top of the authors' own band of earlier data.
- It is a towing-tank test in still liquid, so there was no free-stream turbulence, unlike a wind tunnel (our note).
  The authors give no uncertainty analysis.
- There is one sphere size and no wall correction, and the blockage (1.2 %) is smaller than this project's planned
  3–5 %.
- The Fig. 3 band is drawn, not tabulated, so the values above are read from the figure.

## What it suggests for this project [Proposed]

> **Update 2026-11-02 (same day):** [Brown2003](Brown2003.md) independently **excluded all 44 of these points above
> Re = 5,500** from its curated data set, citing the vibration Roos reported. That confirms point 1 below. Its Eq. (19)
> gives C_D ≈ 0.42–0.445 at this project's Re. That lies inside our reading of the Fig. 3 band (0.38–0.45 at 10⁴,
> 0.40–0.49 at 2×10⁴) and replaces the Morrison-based "0.39–0.45" in point 2.

1. **Table 1 is not a good single-value reference at our Re.** The more useful product is the band of earlier data in
   Fig. 3: about 0.38–0.45 at Re = 10⁴ and 0.40–0.49 at 2×10⁴.
2. **Morrison2013 checked against Roos1971.** Eq. (1) gives 0.393–0.396 at Re = 1–2.5×10⁴.
   - At Re = 10⁴ it lies inside the band, at 2×10⁴ at the band's lower edge, and at 5×10⁴ below the band.
   - Roos's own points are well above it, but those points are flagged by the authors.
   - Read together, the expected C_D at this project's Re is **about 0.39–0.45, not 0.47**, and the reference itself
     is uncertain by roughly ±7–10 %.
   - A measured C_D near 0.47 at Re ≈ 1.5×10⁴ would not by itself confirm the tunnel. It could equally be a reading
     about 10–20 % high, for example from uncorrected blockage or sting drag.
3. **Vibration biases low-force drag readings.** Roos's vibrating shakedown rig gave scattered and apparently high C_D
   at low drag forces. This project's drag is about 1–2 gf, so keep the fan isolated from the balance (own stand,
   flexible coupling; already proposed). Log enough samples to average, and report the scatter.
4. **A sting is acceptable.** It changed the wake's onset of unsteadiness at Re of a few hundred but did not change
   the measured drag. That supports the planned sting mount, although the sting's own drag must still be measured and
   subtracted.
5. **Let the flow settle before averaging.** Roos saw start-up transients of up to 30 % above steady drag. A tunnel's
   speed ramps up slowly, so the effect should be smaller (our inference), but discard the start of each wind-on
   record.
