## D-001: Validate the tunnel with a sphere before trusting other results

- Date: 2026-10-25 (original README)
- Question: How do we know the tunnel and its instruments read true?
- Decision: Measure the drag coefficient of a sphere and compare it with the published value (about 0.47) before any
  other testing. This comparison is the project's headline deliverable.
- Rationale: A known-answer check of the whole chain (airspeed, force, data reduction). The owner's analogy: the 100 MΩ
  reference resistor used to check the STM.
- Alternatives considered: not recorded.
- Evidence / source: original README.
- Status: Accepted. Open detail: the reference Cd must be matched to the test Reynolds number
  ([PROJECT_BACKGROUND.md](PROJECT_BACKGROUND.md#why-reynolds-number-matters-for-the-validation-standard)).
  **2026-11-02: the comparison value "about 0.47" is superseded by D-009** (Brown and Lawler Eq. (19) at each run's
  Re, with a ±10 % band and a trend check). Validating with a sphere still stands.

## D-002: Pitot tube + differential pressure sensor for airspeed; load cell + HX711 for drag

- Date: 2026-10-25 (original README)
- Question: How are airspeed and drag measured?
- Decision: Airspeed from a pitot tube read by a differential pressure sensor; drag from a load cell read by an HX711,
  with signal conditioning from the owner's ECE kit (LM741/LM348, LM317).
- Rationale: Low-cost, buildable instrumentation the owner controls end to end.
- Alternatives considered: not recorded.
- Evidence / source: original README hardware list.
- Status: Accepted as the approach. Specific parts (sensor ranges, load-cell capacity, microcontroller) are **not
  chosen**; see open questions in [PROJECT_STATUS.md](PROJECT_STATUS.md). Note 2026-11-09: the specific parts were
  chosen by the owner on 2026-11-08 in [docs/buy_list.md](docs/buy_list.md) (one SDP810-125Pa, TAL221 100 g load
  cells, a SparkFun HX711; the Elegoo Mega 2560 as the candidate board). The ECE kit's role is open question 2 (proposed:
  the interface board, not the measurement chain). The approach itself is unchanged.

## D-004: Data management: raw data immutable; videos external; no Git LFS yet

- Date: 2026-10-29
- Question: Where do raw data, processed data and large binary files live?
- Decision: `data/raw/` is never edited and is stored byte-for-byte (no line-ending conversion); `data/processed/` is
  regenerated by scripts in `analysis/`; CSV logs are committed directly; videos are kept out of Git and referenced from the
  data catalog; photos are compressed before committing; Git LFS is **not** enabled until a real need appears
  (e.g. CAD files over ~25 MB). Details: [data/README.md](data/README.md).
- Rationale: The expected sensor logs are small text files. LFS adds a dependency for every clone and counts against
  GitHub storage and bandwidth quotas, which is not justified before any large file exists. Video is the only
  foreseeable file type that would exceed GitHub's 100 MB limit.
- Alternatives considered: enable LFS now for images and CAD (deferred); commit everything (rejected: size limits);
  keep all data outside Git (rejected: breaks reproducibility of the analysis).
- Evidence / source: no data or binary files exist yet (inspected 2026-10-29).
- Status: Tentative. A default chosen by the setup agent. Owner to confirm; revisit when the first CAD files or videos exist.

## D-005: Print the tunnel in segments on the Bambu Lab P1S

- Date: 2026-10-30
- Question: How are tunnel parts larger than the P1S build volume (256 × 256 × 256 mm, manufacturer spec) made?
- Decision: The tunnel will be "decently big" (owner's words; test-section size not yet chosen). Parts that do not
  fit the build volume, starting with the contraction, are printed in segments. Segments get alignment holes (for
  pins or dowels) and are joined, probably with glue.
- Rationale: A one-piece printed contraction would cap the test section at about 100 mm, which gives a sphere drag of
  about 1 gf at 10 m/s ([PROJECT_BACKGROUND.md](PROJECT_BACKGROUND.md#printable-contraction-sizes)). A larger tunnel
  gives larger forces, which are easier to measure.
- Alternatives considered: a one-piece print with a ~100 mm test section (rejected: forces too small).
- Design considerations [Proposed, not decided]: keep internal steps at joints as small as possible, and keep joints
  away from the contraction exit, where the flow is fastest and the boundary layer thinnest. The contraction-design
  method in [Bell1988](resources/notes/Bell1988.md) assumes smooth walls with a laminar boundary layer from the inlet.
  Seal seams against leaks. Unknown: filament, wall thickness, segment layout, adhesive.
- Evidence / source: owner statement, 2026-10-30 session.
- Status: Accepted (segmented printing with alignment holes). Joining method (glue): Tentative. Tunnel size: the
  "decently big" intent is superseded by D-008 (100 mm test section, 2026-11-01). Segmented printing still applies,
  because the 250 mm settling chamber and contraction inlet leave no room for walls and flanges on the 256 mm bed.

## D-007: Open-circuit blower tunnel, fan at the front

- Date: 2026-11-01
- Question: Blower (fan at the front, pushing) or suction (fan at the back, pulling)? And is there an exit diffuser?
- Decision: An open-circuit **blower**: "The fan will go at the front. That will be better" (owner). Proposed layout
  (agent): fan → flexible coupling → short wide-angle diffuser with screens → settling chamber (honeycomb + screens) →
  contraction → closed test section → **open exit, no exit diffuser**.
- Rationale:
  - The test section sits near room pressure, so print seams and the sting slot barely leak (the agent's lean since
    2026-10-31).
  - The honeycomb and screens remove the fan's swirl before the test section.
  - The open exit is the agent's proposal. An exit diffuser by Mehta1979's rule (5°, area ratio 2.5) would add about
    7.5 × the test-section height, about 0.75 m at 100 mm, against the owner's target of a tunnel about 1 m long. It
    would roughly halve the fan pressure and power
    ([PROJECT_BACKGROUND.md](PROJECT_BACKGROUND.md#fan-requirement-estimates)).
- Alternatives considered: suction, the shape of the owner's first sketch
  ([docs/sketches/](docs/sketches/README.md)). The fan's turbulence leaves out of the back, and the sketch's sealed
  electronics box under the floor would stop air leaking in through the sting slot. It needs calm room air at the
  inlet and a square-to-round transition before the fan. An exit diffuser is the other alternative: less fan power,
  more length.
- Consequences: the tunnel will not draw mist in by itself, so optional visualization injects mist after the last
  screen ([TODO.md](TODO.md) P2). The settling chamber runs above room pressure, so any removable panel there needs
  latches or strong magnets.
- Evidence / source: [2026-11-01 session log](docs/sessions/2026-11-01-parts-review-and-kit-inventory.md).
- Status: Accepted (blower, fan at the front; owner). Open exit: Tentative (agent's proposal, not objected to, not
  confirmed).

## D-008: 100 mm × 100 mm test section

- Date: 2026-11-01
- Question: What test-section cross-section? It sets the tunnel length, the sphere size, the drag force and the fan.
- Decision: A square 100 mm × 100 mm test section ("lets keep it at 100mm", owner). The owner first targeted about
  1 m in total including the fan, then allowed more ("we can also go more than 1m if needed").
- Rationale (the comparison shown to the owner; estimates, not measurements,
  [PROJECT_BACKGROUND.md](PROJECT_BACKGROUND.md#tunnel-length-budget)):
  - about 1.15–1.3 m long including a 6-inch fan, close to the owner's 1 m target;
  - one 6-inch inline fan should reach about 13 m/s;
  - the smallest fan, print and parts cost of the sizes compared.
- Alternatives considered:
  - 80 mm, which fits 1 m including the fan but has smaller forces;
  - 150 mm (about 1.6–1.9 m, about twice the force, an 8–10 inch fan);
  - 200 mm (about 2–2.5 m, about four times the force, a 10–12 inch fan, a 500 mm settling chamber in many printed
    pieces). The owner raised 200 mm, then chose 100 mm.
- Consequences:
  - The sphere is about 20 mm at 3 % blockage (25 mm at 5 %). Its drag is about 0.9 gf at 10 m/s and 2.0 gf at
    15 m/s (estimate), so the load cell, its zero drift and the sting drag are the hardest part of the project.
    D-005 had rejected a ~100 mm section for this reason; the owner chose it after seeing the force comparison.
  - Re for a 20 mm sphere is about 1.3×10⁴ at 10 m/s, inside the subcritical C_D plateau.
  - Proposed, not decided: contraction ratio 6.25 (250 mm settling chamber), test section 270–290 mm long.
- Evidence / source: [2026-11-01 session log](docs/sessions/2026-11-01-parts-review-and-kit-inventory.md).
- Status: Accepted (100 mm × 100 mm cross-section; owner). Contraction ratio and section lengths: Tentative.

## D-009: Sphere validation reference: Brown and Lawler Eq. (19) at each run's Re, ±10 % band, plus a trend check

- Date: 2026-11-02
- Question: What is the measured sphere C_D compared with? D-001 said "about 0.47", but the planned test Re is about
  1.3–2.5×10⁴ (20–25 mm spheres at 10–15 m/s), and published C_D changes with Re in that range.
- Decision:
  - Compare each run's corrected C_D (blockage and sting drag removed) with Brown and Lawler (2003) Eq. (19), evaluated
    at that run's Re: C_D = (24/Re)(1 + 0.150 Re^0.681) + 0.407/(1 + 8,710/Re).
  - The reference band is about **±10 %**.
  - Runs at several speeds must also show C_D rising with Re as Eq. (19) predicts: about 12 % from Re = 6.6×10³ to
    2.5×10⁴.
  - The band may be tightened later if the original data tables near Re = 10⁴ are obtained.
- Rationale:
  - Eq. (19) is fitted to 480 curated, wall-corrected sphere-drag points. The selection independently excluded
    Roos1971's vibration-affected points above Re = 5,500. It is an explicit formula the analysis script can evaluate
    for every run.
  - Eq. (19) gives 0.42–0.445 for the planned runs. Its own Fig. 3 data sit about 7 % above it at this Re, with about
    ±8 % scatter (our pixel measurement, axes checked against the Stokes region). The paper's "81 % within ±5 %" is an
    average over all Re, so ±10 % is the honest band here.
  - A single number cannot serve, because C_D rises about 12 % across the test range. The trend check gives evidence
    that does not depend on the absolute band.
- Alternatives considered:
  - A single value of 0.47 (Hoerner, used by Weakly2024). It ignores the Re dependence; it falls inside the band, but
    only at the top of it.
  - Morrison2013 Eq. (1): 7–11 % below Eq. (19) and the data at this Re.
  - Eq. (19) with ±5 % and Clift et al. (1978) as a cross-check, the agent's first proposal. Rejected: ±5 % is too
    tight at this Re, and Clift was fitted to almost the same data, so it is not independent.
  - Roos1971 Table 1: its points at this Re read high, per its authors.
- Evidence / source: [Brown2003 notes](resources/notes/Brown2003.md), [Morrison2013 notes](resources/notes/Morrison2013.md),
  [Roos1971 notes](resources/notes/Roos1971.md); this session's log (2026-11-02).
- Status: Accepted (owner, 2026-11-02, choosing the ±10 % + trend version over the ±5 % + Clift version).

## D-010: Design top speed 13 m/s; validation runs from about 6.5 m/s

- Date: 2026-11-08
- Question: What top airspeed is the 100 mm tunnel designed for? It sets the fan, the pressure-sensor range and the
  force available (open question 3).
- Decision: A design top speed of **13 m/s** in the test section, with sphere validation runs from about 6.5 m/s
  upward. Owner: "the 13m/s top speed is a big priority" (2026-11-08), and the fan was chosen on that basis (D-011).
- Rationale (the agent's 2026-11-05 trade-off; estimates,
  [PROJECT_BACKGROUND.md](PROJECT_BACKGROUND.md#top-speed-trade-off-proposed)):
  - D-009's trend check gains little above it: Eq. (19) rises 8.7 % from the 20 mm sphere at 6.5 m/s to the 25 mm
    sphere at 13 m/s, and 9.8 % at 15 m/s;
  - one 6-inch fan listing about 500 Pa should just reach it;
  - q = 101 Pa fits a ±125 Pa pressure sensor (full scale at 14.4 m/s);
  - below about 6.5 m/s the 20 mm sphere's drag is only 0.2–0.3 gf, so fixed zero errors dominate.
- Alternatives considered: 11–12 m/s (smaller fan, about 30 % less force); 15 m/s (about 35 % more force, 1.5× the
  fan power, an 8-inch fan or two fans, a ±249 Pa or ±500 Pa sensor); 20 m/s.
- Consequences: pressure-sensor range ±125 Pa (SDP810-125Pa proposed). The real top speed is measured with the pitot
  once the tunnel runs. If the fan reaches only about 12 m/s, the validation still works with less force.
- Evidence / source: [2026-11-07 session log](docs/sessions/2026-11-07-top-speed-and-fan-search.md); owner's
  statement in the 2026-11-08 session.
- Status: Accepted (owner, 2026-11-08). Note 2026-11-09: the owner asked whether 13 m/s is a must-have. The agent's
  answer: no. The validation works at 11–12 m/s, with less force (71 % at 11 m/s) and a smaller C_D rise for the
  trend check (7.8 % against 9.2 %). If the VEVOR fan is chosen (D-011, reopened), the top speed becomes whatever it
  reaches, about 11 m/s by estimate.

## D-011: Fan: AC Infinity CLOUDLINE S6 (model AI-CLS6)

- Date: 2026-11-08
- Question: Which fan drives the tunnel (PROJECT_STATUS §3)?
- Decision: The **AC Infinity CLOUDLINE S6, model AI-CLS6**, with the 10-speed controller it comes with, on its own
  stand. Its collar, wrapped in one layer of neoprene so it never touches the print, slides snugly into a printed
  diffuser inlet made as small as that allows, and duct tape seals the joint (owner's design, 2026-11-08). Owner: "no
  other fan gives us the exact
  maximum static pressure rating other than the cloudline s6 … going with the S6 is our best bet" (2026-11-08).
  Not bought yet; see [docs/buy_list.md](docs/buy_list.md).
- Rationale:
  - The maker's page (read 2026-11-08): 425 CFM, 503 Pa static pressure (2.02 inH₂O), a PWM-controlled EC motor,
    10 speed levels, dual ball bearings, 100–240 V AC, 70 W maximum, 32.0 cm long, $99.00. Retailers list the same
    fan as the "CLOUDLINE PRO S6" at 402 CFM.
  - It is the only candidate whose published figures can be checked against 13 m/s. Straight-line fan-curve estimate:
    13.5–14.1 m/s at 425 CFM, 13.1–13.7 m/s at 402 CFM. If the real curve gives only 80–90 % of the straight-line
    flow, 12.0–13.3 m/s. AC Infinity publishes no fan curve.
- Alternatives considered: VIVOSUN AeroZesh S6 (no static pressure published); the VEVOR, Skyshalo and HFS 6-inch AC
  fans (no pressure listed, or about 300 Pa, which gives about 11–12 m/s); two Delta PFR1212UHE-SP00 server fans (very
  loud, about 12 m/s, 5.4 A at 12 V).
- Consequences:
  - The diffuser's inlet collar is printed to the fan's outlet collar, measured once the fan arrives (the maker gives
    the duct size as 6" but not the collar diameter).
  - Speed is set in 10 steps with the supplied controller. Hobbyists report driving AC Infinity fans with a 10 V PWM
    signal on the UIS port, which could let the Arduino menu set the speed without touching mains (open question 11).
    No source found confirms the pinout; not relied on.
- Evidence / source: [AC Infinity product page](https://acinfinity.com/hydroponics-growers/ventilation/inline-fan-systems/cloudline-pro-s6-quiet-inline-duct-fan-system-with-speed-controller-6-inch/)
  (read 2026-11-08); estimate script in this session's log.
- Status: **Tentative since 2026-11-09.** Accepted by the owner on 2026-11-08; reopened the next day, when the owner
  found the VEVOR VV-GDFJ-AC6 (400 CFM, 300 Pa listed, AC motor with a speed controller) at $53.90. The owner will
  choose between the two on budget: "put either or, i havent ordered anything yet" (2026-11-09).
  - Estimates (straight-line fan curves, five window screens): the VEVOR reaches about 11.3 m/s (10.8 at 90 % of its
    curve), the S6 about 13.0–13.4 m/s (12.3–12.7).
  - The VEVOR gives about 73 % of the S6's force at top speed. Its figures are the seller's.
  - Both validate. The fan-to-tunnel joint, the stand and the pressure sensor suit either.
  - Record the final choice as a new decision that supersedes this one. Purchase: To buy.

## D-012: No on-tunnel display or knob; all output on the laptop

- Date: 2026-11-08
- Question: Does the tunnel need its own display and knob menu (planned by the owner on 2026-11-01, "like the menu for
  an Ender 3 with a knob")?
- Decision: No. Owner: "i think we dont need a display and knob. this isnt a device, so any output we need lets just
  run on the computer" (2026-11-08). The Arduino streams readings over USB to the laptop, which logs them (as already
  planned) and shows them live.
- Rationale: the tunnel is a lab rig used with the laptop beside it, not a stand-alone device. Dropping the display
  removes a part to buy, its wiring, and a firmware task.
- Alternatives considered: a 128×64 "Full Graphic Smart Controller" with knob, buzzer and SD slot (about $15–24), or a
  20×4 I²C LCD plus a KY-040 encoder (about $12–15); both from the 2026-11-08 buy-list review.
- Consequences:
  - Supersedes the 2026-11-01 plan for an on-tunnel display and knob menu (PROJECT_STATUS §1 objectives, TODO P2
    "Firmware with an on-tunnel display and knob menu", experimental_setup "Display and controls").
  - Live view: the Arduino IDE's Serial Plotter to start with; a laptop script later if needed.
  - The interface board needs no display or encoder headers.
  - Setting the fan speed from a menu (open question 11) is no longer a goal. The fan keeps its own controller.
- Evidence / source: owner's statement in the 2026-11-08 session; [docs/buy_list.md](docs/buy_list.md) §8.
- Status: Accepted (owner, 2026-11-08).
